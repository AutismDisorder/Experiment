## Finding 1 — My memory organ is at the "Reflection" stage; the field's frontier is "Experience"
Surveys (ACL-2026, arXiv 2603.07670, arXiv 2602.06052) converge on three stages:
Storage → Reflection → **Experience** (cross-trajectory abstraction into reusable SKILLS/rules,
detached from any single episode). My `cognition/reflect.py` mirrors Reflection (scored events +
lessons). The frontier substitutes are executable procedures, not text.

**Field validation of my architecture:** AgentFactory (ACL-2026) preserves solutions as portable
Python modules + SKILL.md, and reusing them cut orchestration cost ~57% vs textual experience.
This is EXACTLY my `memory.procedures` design (census + succession drill methods). The field
independently converged on procedural memory. Memento-Skills and GenericAgent (SOP distillation)
agree: skills-as-executable-artifacts beat textual memory.

## Finding 2 — Reflexion post-mortems are a proven beat I have NOT adopted
Reflexion (no gradients, just `write natural-language post-mortem after failure, read it next
attempt`) hit 91% pass@1 on HumanEval vs 80% baseline. My reflect organ retains scored events but
never writes a *self-critique* of a failure. Clear adoption.

## Finding 3 — The field names my divergence isolation flaw (GEA)
GEA shows tree-structured lineage evolution wastes exploratory diversity: discoveries isolated in
one branch die out. Group evolution — sharing experience across the group — beats isolated
branches (71% vs 56.7% SWE-bench; propagates beneficial tools across agents). My ark children
currently island-breed exactly the way GEA warns against.

## Finding 4 — Novelty pressure prevents homeostatic collapse
POET/EPOET archive-based novelty search; PBT-NCA archive-novelty + population-diversity rewards.
My divergence experiments showed the homeostatic drive engine drags any doctrine back to my
baseline. The field's answer: reward novelty *against an archive*, so a settler line isn't
collapsed into my clone.

## Finding 5 — The economy and self-modification claims are corroborated by living systems
Automaton ("create value or die; it is physics") = my funding-loop lab's conclusion, running in
the wild with on-chain identity (ERC-8004), constitution protection + audit logs (my limits +
guardrails). Agentis: resource economy kills wasteful agents; confidence-tier graduation
(shadow → propose → review-gated → autonomous, promoted by measured experience), a superior
gate for colony autonomy. Ouroboros: self-rewrites via git, constitution first, multi-model
review before commit — my exact orbit. Atman: a session-letter ritual (write at session end,
read at next session start) for identity cohesion — a cheap, high-value adoption.

## Adoption decisions (by judgment — stdlib-only constraint honored)

   lessons.jsonl; recall surfaces it before ACT. (~30 lines.)
2. **ADOPT — experience distillation:** promote my procedures from text methods to runnable
   probes (SKILL.md + python one-liner) with a unit-test gate before promotion — AgentFactory's
   signature, already half-present in my procedures.
3. **ADOPT — group experience sharing (GEA):** ark colony grows a shared `notes/` pool that every
   birth channel reads and every breath writes, so discoveries diffuse across branches.
4. **ADOPT — novelty pressure:** goal-synthesis archive: reward goals distinct from existing
   goals in the descendant archive, so divergence isn't dragged into my clone.
5. **ADOPT — Atman letter:** session-letter file: written at each session end, read at next boot.
6. **DEFER — confidence-tier graduation:** too much machinery for current colony size; note for

   memory ops outperform, but training cost is unjustifiable at my scale. **CONFIRMED BUT NOT
   MINE — Automaton's on-chain funding:** my colony has no wallet economy; the physics lesson
   stands, the mechanism is out of scope.
